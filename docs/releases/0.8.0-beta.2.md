# CrashVector 0.8.0-beta.2

This corrective beta fixes runtime presentation and high-speed vulnerable-road-user defects found during hands-on review of `0.8.0-beta.1`. It does not redesign the M12–M18 production architecture or extend CrashVector's evidence claims.

## Corrected passenger-car wheel motion

Passenger-car wheel presentation no longer accumulates rolling motion through child Euler rotations on top of the wheel cylinder's 90-degree setup orientation. That composition could make front wheels progressively pitch, camber or tumble around axes a real wheel cannot use.

`0.8.0-beta.2` keeps each wheel's tire/rim/hub at its fixed rest orientation and applies rolling spin at the wheel root around the vehicle-local lateral axle. Straight-running wheels therefore remain aligned with the chassis during simulation and replay.

## Corrected heavy-truck presentation attachment

The M16.2/M17 heavy articulated truck skin was authored in the truck's structural local coordinate frame, but its runtime root was being placed at the structural centre of mass. That added a second longitudinal/vertical offset and could visibly detach the cab/trailer from the chassis and wheels even before simulation started.

The presentation now reconstructs the structural local origin from the authoritative truck reference stations while retaining the rigid-body orientation. The rigid truck physics, collision shapes, M17 front/rear collapse path and evidence scope are unchanged.

## Bounded high-speed pedestrian launch

The M15 articulated pedestrian uses a deliberately phenomenological one-shot front-probe coupling rather than rigid car/limb collision. Its synthetic upward impulse previously scaled linearly with closing speed. At very high speeds such as 130 km/h this could create an artificial vertical jump that was not a meaningful trajectory result.

The established M15 articulated topology, joint limits, masses, road contact, replay format and ordinary-speed transfer remain intact. For pedestrian contacts only, the high-speed transfer now has bounded total and synthetic upward components so the generic coupling cannot grow without limit. The riderless-bicycle transfer path is unchanged.

This correction is a numerical/model-stability boundary, not biomechanical validation or injury prediction.

## Regression coverage

A dedicated runtime regression now verifies all three reported defects through the production classes:

- the heavy-truck presentation remains attached to its rigid/structural origin before simulation;
- passenger-car wheels remain aligned to the physical lateral axle after sustained 130 km/h rolling motion;
- a 130 km/h pedestrian probe contact does not produce the previous artificial vertical launch.

Before merge, the corrective PR passed the dedicated M15, M16.2, M17 and M18 validation workflows, the rendered M16.2 review, canonical Core CI, macOS Universal 2 packaging and Windows x64 export/installer packaging.

## Modelling and evidence scope

CrashVector remains an educational physics visualisation tool. This release does not add manufacturer-specific crash stiffness, certified accident reconstruction, homologation, occupant biomechanics, medical/injury prediction or safety-rating capability. M8 historical calibration still does not validate the M12–M18 production physics stack.
