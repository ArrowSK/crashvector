# CrashVector 0.7.0-beta.1

This beta packages M15 and M16 together: articulated vulnerable-road-user dynamics plus the desktop UX/vehicle-visual overhaul.

## M15 — articulated pedestrian and bicycle dynamics

- Pedestrian production targets now use an 11-body articulated rigid chain connected by 10 bounded `Generic6DOFJoint3D` constraints instead of one rigid mannequin.
- Riderless bicycles use a rigid frame plus two independently simulated wheel bodies joined at the hubs.
- The passenger-car front probe can recognize contact with any owned articulated road-user body.
- Vulnerable-target contact impulse is distributed through the articulated target instead of relying on the earlier fixed tumble-torque approximation.
- Replay records/restores articulated part transforms and velocities.
- The final M15 pedestrian implementation deliberately uses `Generic6DOFJoint3D`: an earlier ConeTwist implementation proved numerically unstable under high-speed impact in Godot 4.4.1.
- Dedicated stability gates cover topology, material trajectory, direct-joint motion, passenger-car vertical/rebound behaviour, bicycle wheel motion and a 22 m/s target-centre-of-mass speed sanity ceiling in the 60 km/h regression.

Final 60 km/h regression measurements:

- pedestrian: 11 bodies / 10 joints, 2.51 m/s final centre-of-mass speed, 13.97 m maximum centre-of-mass travel, 105.2° maximum direct-joint motion, 0.001 m maximum passenger-car vertical rise and no measured reverse launch;
- riderless city bicycle: 3 bodies / 2 hub joints, 9.21 m/s final centre-of-mass speed, 19.22 m maximum centre-of-mass travel, 34.85 rad/s maximum wheel angular motion and 0.001 m maximum passenger-car vertical rise.

These are numerical project regressions, not biomechanical validation bands.

## M16 — task-focused desktop UX

The normal crash-building workflow is reorganized around four jobs instead of the accumulated M10 control stack:

- **Scenario builder** for primary vehicle, target and impact speed;
- **3D viewport** as the central working area, with camera/display controls inside it;
- **Properties** for the selected object, with solver/contact settings behind **Advanced setup**;
- **Playback dock** for replay, timeline, analysis and video export.

Secondary commands are moved out of the primary-action cluster, while existing file, calibration/evidence, updater, replay, analysis and export behaviour remains available.

The established M10 shell-region identifiers remain in place so the historical responsive-layout tests continue to protect the desktop floor.

## Class-specific passenger-car visuals

M16 replaces the visibly scaled-hatchback presentation with generated class-specific visual profiles for generic A, B, C, D, J and M passenger-car classes.

- City car, small hatchback, compact, midsize, SUV/crossover and MPV/minivan now differ materially in stance and silhouette.
- SUV profiles use a taller body, higher bonnet, larger wheel package and lower cladding.
- MPV profiles use a cab-forward, extended-greenhouse layout.
- D-segment profiles sit lower and longer than the B-segment baseline.
- The presentation skin reads the existing deforming structural model every frame.
- Legacy shell/wheel presentation is hidden only while the M16 skin is active.

The new visual geometry does not alter rigid collision shapes, mass, stiffness, structural beams, crush behaviour, contact probes or solver settings. These are generic presentation archetypes, not branded production models or homologation geometry.

## Production integration and regression gates

The M16 production scene preserves M15 rather than falling back to the M14 road-user proxy. Its regression explicitly verifies that selecting a pedestrian through the real desktop UI produces `RoadUserArticulatedProxy3D` with the finalized articulated topology and isolated collision channels.

Release acceptance requires the existing architecture audit, project import/parse, Core M0–M12 regression suite, dedicated M10, M11, M12, M13, M14, M15 and M16 validation workflows, macOS Universal 2 packaging and the Windows x64 installer lifecycle.

## Modelling and evidence scope

CrashVector remains an educational physics visualisation tool.

- Pedestrian/bicycle output is contact/trajectory simulation only. M15 does not add injury prediction, HIC/AIS, tissue loading, validated dummy behaviour, survivability estimates or a bicycle rider model.
- M16 vehicle skins are presentation geometry only and do not represent manufacturer-specific crash behaviour.
- Rigid lorry and motorcycle production simulation remain blocked until those targets are ported to the rigid-body world architecture.
- Visual Compare and Comparison Lab remain unavailable in the production path until their historical synchronous runner is replaced by a rigid-body scene recorder.
- Historical M8 calibration does not validate the M12–M16 rigid-body, staged-collapse, articulated-road-user or presentation path.

See `docs/M15_ARTICULATED_ROAD_USERS.md`, `docs/M16_UX_AND_VEHICLE_VISUALS.md`, `docs/CALIBRATION.md` and `docs/PHYSICS.md` for details.
