# CrashVector 0.8.0-beta.1

This beta packages the completed M17 and M18 production work on top of the M16.1 desktop presentation release. It preserves the established M12–M16 rigid-body, staged-deformation, vulnerable-road-user and presentation architecture while extending production vehicle-pair behavior and restoring production Comparison.

## M17 — reciprocal impacts and production comparison

- Visual Compare and Comparison Lab now execute each requested variant in an isolated production `SubViewport` / `World3D` using the current Godot `RigidBody3D` scene rather than presenting the historical reduced-order comparison runner as current physics.
- Supported dynamic vehicle targets may begin ahead of or behind the passenger car. Striker/struck direction follows positions, headings and velocities rather than the UI labels “primary” and “target”.
- Passenger cars gain bounded direct rear-impact deformation while retaining the existing M12/M13 front-crush and staged whole-body failure paths.
- Heavy articulated trucks gain bounded local front/rear collapse with physical collision-face retreat. The truck remains one rigid world assembly rather than a tractor/fifth-wheel/trailer articulation model.
- Generic rigid lorry / box truck and riderless motorcycle are ported to production Godot `RigidBody3D` world motion instead of remaining blocked or falling back to the historical solver.
- The proving road is extended to approximately 4 km × 20 m for long and high-speed production scenarios.
- Reciprocal deformation demand retains both reduced-mass relative-velocity energy and measured Godot contact impulse reconstructed as `J² / (2μ)`.

## M18 — passenger-car side impacts

- Passenger-car pairs support arbitrary relative headings, including perpendicular T-bone layouts.
- Real lateral Godot contact samples drive bounded generic side deformation on the struck passenger car.
- The struck protected-cell collision face retreats laterally with intrusion, so the lateral response is physical rather than presentation-only.
- Existing longitudinal deformation paths remain intact: M12/M13 front crush and M17 rear crush are preserved.
- Replay stores independent left/right lateral crush state and side-contact energy, and production metrics expose lateral intrusion.
- Broadside support is deliberately limited to passenger-car pairs. Heavy-truck, rigid-lorry, motorcycle and bicycle broadside cases remain blocked until corresponding lateral/contact models are implemented.

The final M18 PR head passed the dedicated M10–M18 workflow chain, canonical Core CI and both native packaging gates. The merged M18 `main` commit `77fae8d6058724c54de1e9003020fccfddaa63b7` subsequently passed M17 and M18 dedicated checks again together with Core CI and native packaging.

This release version bump does not weaken or replace those production regressions. The normal release workflow must still build and verify the macOS Universal 2 DMG and Windows x64 installer before publishing `v0.8.0-beta.1`.

## Modelling and evidence scope

CrashVector remains an educational physics visualisation tool. M17/M18 do not add manufacturer-specific crash stiffness, certified accident reconstruction, homologation, occupant biomechanics, injury prediction or safety-rating capability.

The M18 side-impact stiffness/intrusion model is generic and phenomenological. M17 rigid-lorry and motorcycle support provides production rigid-body world motion but not detailed crush models for those targets. Heavy-truck fifth-wheel articulation, non-passenger-car broadside models, cyclist coupling and moving pedestrians remain outside the implemented production scope.

Historical M8 calibration remains a separate reduced-order correlation path and does not validate the M12–M18 production stack.
