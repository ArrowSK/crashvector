# CrashVector 0.9.0-beta.11

This corrective M24 desktop beta fixes the production pedestrian launch defect found after `0.9.0-beta.10` and makes the motorcycle and generic vehicle presentation visibly reflect the deformation and presentation work already present in the source. The physics/contact architecture remains the existing CrashVector rigid-body production path; this release does not add manufacturer-specific crashworthiness or biomechanics claims.

- Production pedestrian preview rebuilds now synchronously tear down the previous articulated sibling bodies and joints before the replacement rig is created. This removes the stale-rig overlap that could inject a large solver impulse before the vehicle reached the pedestrian.
- The production pedestrian regression now requires a real vehicle contact and bounds centre-of-mass and articulated-part vertical launch/height growth on the actual desktop scene.
- At the validated 50 km/h production regression, the pedestrian records real contact with a 3.01 m/s maximum centre-of-mass vertical speed, 0.16 m maximum centre-height rise, 6.68 m/s maximum articulated-part vertical speed and 0.82 m maximum part-height rise.
- Motorcycle structural deformation is now visibly legible in the presentation layer: fork, swingarm, tank, seat and related visual details follow the existing bounded front/rear/side structural deformation state.
- Passenger-car presentation keeps the previously validated Kenney Car Kit mapping; no contact-envelope changes were introduced to force a more detailed SUV mesh.
- The generic rigid lorry can use the pinned CC0 Kenney delivery-truck body through a presentation-only fit adapter while its existing CrashVector collision, mass and deformation model remains authoritative.
- Articulated-truck, motorcycle, pedestrian/bicycle and generic tracked-vehicle/tank presentation receive additional authored visual detail without redefining their existing production collision envelopes or solver authority.
- The pinned Kenney Car Kit remains the only third-party vehicle presentation source used by these changes and stays covered by the existing CC0 attribution/provenance documentation.

Validation for this release includes the full M0-M23 regression stack on the release candidate, the dedicated production pedestrian stability regression, the 50 km/h motorcycle contact/deformation regression, and the normal macOS Universal 2 / Windows x64 package, checksum and update-manifest gates.

CrashVector remains an educational visualisation. Pedestrian, bicycle, cyclist and motorcycle-rider behaviour is not a biomechanics or injury model; heavy-vehicle/tank deformation and articulation remain generic educational approximations rather than manufacturer or regulatory validation.
