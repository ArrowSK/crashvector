# CrashVector 0.3.0-beta.1

M11 rebuilds the production crush/contact dynamics that sit underneath the M10 presentation layer. The change is intentionally mechanical rather than cosmetic: symmetric frontal impacts now load a compliant multi-point contact surface and propagate force through a higher-resolution front structure with progressive plastic shortening and bending/fold constraints.

## What changed

- Production passenger cars retain the historical seven reference stations and add four engine-bay cross-sections, increasing the production structural model to 44 nodes.
- Distinct bumper/nose, crash-box, front-rail, upper-rail, subframe/cross-member and firewall-transition components.
- Progressive post-yield beam force curves and plastic bending constraints.
- Stronger passenger-cell angular constraints plus a longitudinal anti-inversion guard so centred frontal loading cannot numerically flip the protected-cell reference frame through 180 degrees.
- Static wall/barrier/pole/tree contact is force-based inside the same solver substep as structural response; normal contact no longer instantaneously stops/reverses nodes or performs large penetration correction.
- Vehicle-pair contact is force-based and expands historical two-node seeds to a full structural impact face before multi-point matching.
- Contact damping follows configured restitution; structural and contact damping are bounded against the relative motion available in each explicit substep.
- Public solver-substep validation and the desktop Physics control now support 1–32 substeps. The unchanged stored M8 56.5 km/h reference uses 32 substeps for reference-quality convergence of the refined structure; its evidence and regression corridors are not loosened.
- New M11 regressions check front shortening, cell preservation, left/right symmetry, centred-impact yaw, permanent fold angle, multipoint contact, momentum conservation and high-speed numerical stability.

## Scope

CrashVector remains a reduced-order educational simulator. M11 improves mechanical plausibility and removes a known impulse/pivot failure mode, but generic classes are not production-vehicle finite-element models and M11 does not broaden the evidence claim. Existing calibration/evidence labels continue to distinguish reference-correlated, near-reference, class-scaled and extrapolated scenarios.
