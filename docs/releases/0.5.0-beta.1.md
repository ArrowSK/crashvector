# CrashVector 0.5.0-beta.1

M13 corrects the remaining severe-frontal-impact limitation in the M12 hybrid model. M12 stabilised whole-vehicle motion by moving translation, rotation, gravity, road support and collision response to Godot `RigidBody3D`, but it still treated the passenger cell behind the front crush zone as effectively indestructible. At extreme speeds that meant roughly the first metre of the car could collapse while the firewall, roof and passenger compartment retained an implausibly unchanged shape.

## What changed

- The stable M12 rigid-body world-motion architecture remains authoritative. M13 does not return whole-car motion to the historical point-mass structural solver.
- Frontal structural failure is now staged. Normal impacts first consume the bumper, crash boxes, rails and engine-bay crush zone. If collision demand remains after that zone is substantially exhausted, deformation can propagate into the firewall/cowl, floor and rockers, A-pillars and roof, then into wider passenger-cell shortening and rear-body buckling at extreme demand.
- Stage activation is based on normal collision energy and actual measured front-crush travel, not on a hard-coded vehicle speed threshold. Dynamic rigid-body targets use relative normal closing speed and reduced mass.
- The front crush zone remains limited to its physical design travel instead of being stretched indefinitely. Additional demand is routed into later structural zones.
- The protected-cell rigid collision face retreats as catastrophic structural collapse develops. Severe cabin shortening therefore creates additional real world travel against the obstacle instead of being only a visual mesh deformation inside an unchanged invisible rigid box.
- The deformable painted shell already follows the structural stations, so firewall intrusion, A-pillar/roof movement, floor/rocker distortion and rear buckling are visible in the normal 3D view.
- Replay state now carries the M13 failure-stage values so recorded severe crashes retain the staged deformation state.

## Regression results

The M13 regressions use generic B-class project parameters. They are project stability/plausibility checks, not manufacturer or regulatory crash-test predictions.

For the 50 km/h centred rigid-wall preservation case, the release branch records approximately:

- 105.0 kJ peak normal collision energy;
- 0.536 m front crush;
- 0.000 m firewall intrusion;
- 0.000 m passenger-cell collapse;
- 0.000 m rear-body buckle.

This preserves the intended M12 behaviour: a moderate frontal crash uses the engineered front crush zone without arbitrarily destroying the cabin.

For the 200 km/h centred rigid-wall severe-failure case, the release branch records approximately:

- 1,739.2 kJ peak normal collision energy;
- 0.945 m front-zone crush;
- 0.300 m firewall intrusion;
- 0.820 m passenger-cell collapse;
- 0.231 m rear-body buckle;
- 1.948 m combined longitudinal structural collapse;
- 0.015 m/s maximum reverse speed;
- 0.005 m maximum chassis vertical rise;
- 0.89 degrees maximum pitch.

The important regression property is not those exact deformation numbers. It is that a severe impact can no longer stop structurally after the nose is exhausted, while the M12 no-launch/no-jump rigid-body behaviour remains intact.

## Modelling scope

The staged capacities and collapse curves are phenomenological generic CrashVector parameters. They are not finite-element material models, manufacturer-specific body-in-white data, injury predictions, or evidence that a particular production vehicle would deform by these exact amounts.

M13 remains an educational visualisation model. The correct interpretation is comparative: increasing collision demand can progressively defeat more structural zones instead of hitting an artificial indestructible passenger-cell boundary.

Rigid lorry, motorcycle, bicycle and pedestrian production simulation remain temporarily blocked until they are ported to the rigid-body world-motion architecture. Visual Compare and Comparison Lab also remain temporarily unavailable rather than silently invoking the historical world-motion solver.
